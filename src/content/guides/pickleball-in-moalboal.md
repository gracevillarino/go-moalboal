Pickleball arrived in Moalboal as a small, mostly expat pastime and has since become one of the more visible non-diving activities in town. Cebu's first dedicated court was built here by Massimo Corti, and local organisers say the game has since spread from Moalboal down the coast to Santander and up to Bogo.

## Where to play

- **Beyond Island Resort** — an outdoor hard court on Bantayan 1 Street, near Basdiot and Panagsama Beach. It is open daily from around 9am to 9pm, with a small donation rather than a fixed fee.
- **Kenny Dink Moalboal** — a court-booking setup with its own schedule; check current availability and rates directly rather than assuming a walk-in game.
- **Tropical Pickleball Moalboal** — a court on Tubli Road, listed on court-finder sites; confirm hours and fees before heading over since listings are not always current.

Court counts, hours and prices change as the scene grows, so treat this as a starting list rather than a guarantee — message a venue or the local Facebook group ("Pickleball Moalboal Cebu") before making a special trip.

## What to expect

Courts here are informal by design: expect a hard outdoor surface, a portable or permanent net, and a mix of long-term expats, balikbayans and increasingly, Filipino locals who picked up the game in the last couple of years. Bring your own paddle if you have one — gear-sharing at a small beach town court is limited, and turning up with your own equipment means you are not waiting on someone else's set.

## Heat, timing and etiquette

Moalboal sits close to the equator, so mid-afternoon sessions get brutally hot on an open-air hard court. Morning and early evening slots are more comfortable and tend to be when regular groups play — arriving around a known session time is more reliable than showing up alone at midday and hoping for a game.

As with any small-town sport scene, courts are shared community spaces rather than commercial facilities with staff on-site. Confirm any donation or fee expectation before playing, keep noise reasonable near residential areas, and let regulars set the rotation if you are new — dropping in and demanding a court is a fast way to be unwelcome twice.

> The sport's growth outward from Moalboal to Santander and Bogo suggests this is not a passing fad — but the courts themselves are still informal, community-run spaces. Treat them that way.
