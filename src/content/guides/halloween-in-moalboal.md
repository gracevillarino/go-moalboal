Late October in Moalboal pulls in two directions at once. Across the Philippines, October 31 through November 2 is Undas — the country's real, deeply-rooted observance of All Saints' and All Souls' Day, built around family, cemeteries and remembrance rather than costumes. At the same time, Panagsama Beach's small backpacker-and-expat bar strip has picked up the Western habit of a themed, costumed party night. Both are genuinely happening in the same town in the same week, and it helps to know which one you're walking into.

## Undas: the tradition that actually runs the week

Undas is the Filipino term for All Saints' Day (November 1) and All Souls' Day (November 2), rooted in Spanish-era Catholic tradition and layered over older Filipino beliefs about ancestors and spirits. Families clean and repaint graves, light candles, bring flowers and food, and often camp out at the cemetery for the day — treating it as a reunion with relatives, living and dead, rather than a solemn, silent occasion.

For visitors, the practical effect is that Moalboal quiets down in places you might not expect. Staff at businesses, dive shops and homestays frequently travel to their home province or barangay to be with family for the holiday, so expect thinner staffing, shorter hours or short closures around November 1–2, especially at smaller, locally-run places. This is a bigger deal locally than any bar-scene Halloween party, and it's worth planning around rather than being surprised by.

## Where the costume-party side shows up

The Western, costume-party version of Halloween in Moalboal lives almost entirely on Panagsama Beach's short strip of backpacker bars, not in the town centre. Three of that strip's regulars:

- **[Isla Bar](/directory/isla-bar)** — has an established habit of running themed nights, masquerade and neon parties among them, as part of its regular weekend lineup. It's the closest thing to a standing "dress up and go out" tradition in town.
- **[Blue Mango Bar - Restaurant](/directory/blue-mango-bar-restaurant)** — part of the same after-dark strip, with DJs and live music alongside its restaurant service.
- **[Haze Bar](/directory/haze-bar)** — another Panagsama regular, drawing the same mixed crowd of long-term expats, divers coming off a day boat, and backpackers passing through.

None of this is centrally organised, and a specific Halloween-night party is not something that gets locked in and announced months ahead. Bars post their own themed-night schedules on Facebook and Instagram in the days leading up to the date, so the reliable move is to check a bar's page in the last week of October rather than assume there's a set annual event — the same way you'd confirm court hours before a [pickleball](/explore/pickleball-in-moalboal) session, or a dive slot before turning up at a shop.

## Going out over Undas week

A few things are worth knowing if you're joining a costume night during this particular week:

- **Keep it to the bar strip.** Panagsama's party scene is contained and low-key by design — a loud, costumed group wandering into the town centre or near a cemetery during Undas will land very differently than the same group on Isla Bar's dance floor.
- **Expect thinner services elsewhere.** Motorbike rental shops, laundry services and smaller eateries may run reduced hours around November 1–2 as staff travel home — plan errands and dive bookings a day or two either side of the holiday.
- **Confirm the date, not just the season.** "Halloween" as a bar event tends to mean the closest weekend to October 31, not the date itself — message the venue directly if a specific night matters to your plans.

> Undas is the tradition Moalboal actually stops for. The costume party on Panagsama's bar strip is real, but it's a backpacker-scene overlay on top of that week — worth enjoying, but not what the date means to the town around it.
